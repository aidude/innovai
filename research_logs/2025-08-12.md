# Implementation Log - August 12, 2025

1. Temporal Displacement as a Feature: Instead of predicting what comes immediately next, what if we trained models to predict tokens that appear 1000, 10000, or variable distances away? This could capture long-range semantic attractors - the "gravity wells" that pull narratives toward certain outcomes. Imagine training on Wikipedia where the model learns that mentioning "Marie Curie" early in an article creates loss pressure toward "Nobel Prize" appearing much later, even through completely different intermediate paths.
2. Negative Space Modeling: Most recommendation systems focus on what people did click/buy/read. But what about modeling the vast space of what they almost clicked but didn't? The items that appeared in their peripheral vision, the rabbit holes they resisted. This negative space might contain richer information about true preferences versus convenience.
3. Cross-Modal Serendipity Bridges: What if we trained models to predict not just distant tokens within a document, but distant modalities? Input a text snippet, predict what music someone would listen to while reading it. Input a purchase, predict what architectural photos they'd find compelling. These cross-modal leaps might capture deeper personality/aesthetic patterns.
4. Collaborative Filtering for Ideas: Instead of user-item interactions, what about concept-concept interactions across all human-generated text? Which philosophical ideas tend to co-occur with which scientific discoveries across decades of writing, but separated by many tokens/documents?
